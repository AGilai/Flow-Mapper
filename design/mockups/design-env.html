Design environments: an HLD for the SASE project

Revision 4: frames 1–9 approved and built (0.24); frame 10 adds references (🔒), importing one level into a design, and partial-flow warnings. Frame 11, decided (rev 5): a pastel colour per component on heads and activation bars; arrows keep the transport colour (option A); colours assigned per environment and stored, so a component keeps its colour and there are no clashes up to 8; past 8 the palette repeats; neutral for client apps, externals and data stores. Static mockup for the maintainer. A design environment is a normal Flow-Mapper environment that holds a high-level design. You start it from any environment (prod, or another design), edit it on the canvas through shared drafts, sketch at subsystem level and refine down to components and endpoints, pull in what's missing from other environments while you work, and discuss any item. Names are illustrative. Tokens, chips, toolbar, tabs and the docked panel are the app's own (0.19.1).

  1. Status is computed, not stored. Every environment keeps the revision it was copied from (its baseline). The server compares the current state with the baseline at read time and marks each item New, Changed, Removed, or nothing (unchanged, "From prod" in the meta line).
  2. On every level. Subsystem link, component, endpoint, call, queue, message, a field of a message, a field of a collection. The mark goes on the highest new item only: a new component's endpoints are new by implication and don't repeat the icon, so the page doesn't fill with stars.
  3. The new icon is a small yellow spark, with one short twinkle when the page loads (off with reduced motion). It's the only yellow in the app: --warn stays amber and is never shown as a star.
  4. Words. Environment › subsystem › domain (later) › component › endpoint / message › field. A flow has a name ("Agent connects to POP"); the entry key is secondary. A draft is a named, shared set of changes that is applied in one step.
  5. Harvested environments stay read-only. Edit mode, drafts and import exist only in design environments.

Frame 6. Importing what's missing while you work

Even a design that started Blank can pull in components and endpoints from other environments when it needs them. Three ways in: the assistant notices while drafting, a call points at something this environment lacks, or you search.

  1. The assistant checks as it drafts and asks once, with a short list: what matches, what exists elsewhere (import?), what exists nowhere (add as new). It never imports without asking.
  2. A call to something missing shows a neutral notice (not a problem: in a design it's normal) with "Import from prod" when another environment has it.
  3. Import from another environment searches every environment, groups results by environment, and says what comes along. Imports go into the open draft, so they're reviewed and applied like any edit. Their baseline is the source's current revision.
  4. Imported, not new. An imported endpoint is unchanged against its own source, so it gets no spark. The meta line says where it came from ("from prod, imported Oct 22").

Frame 11. A colour per component (decided: option A, stored per environment)

Maintainer: "each component shall have its own colour on flows, so when jumping over the API it is clear what the in and out component is; same for its activation bar. I'm doubting about the link itself." Today every service head has a black stripe and every bar takes the colour of the call that reaches it, so a flow reads as lines of colour between identical black boxes. The idea: component colour says who, transport colour says how. Pastel fills, a deeper tone of the same hue only for the stripe and the bar outline.

  1. Who vs how. The head and the activation bar carry the component colour; the line, its arrowhead, the kind chip and channels (topics, queues) keep the transport colour. UI-GUIDE §2 and §8 stay true for everything that is a connection.
  2. Eight pastel slots, each tested against all ten transport tokens, status, --new and --accent in both themes. The hue wheel is already crowded by transports, so the colours are muted: most sit at least 7 apart from every token; cyan and ochre are the weak slots (4 to 6), see the table.
  3. Stable per name. Slot = hash(component name) mod 8, the same on every page. Two components of one flow on the same slot: the one whose name sorts later takes the next free slot, so the result doesn't depend on step order. Collisions are likely (about half of four-component flows), see open question.
  4. Who gets colour. Services, gateways, functions, workers: yes. Client apps and third-party externals: neutral grey, they are outside the system. Data stores: neutral. Topics and queues: transport colour, they are channels, not components.
  5. Nested bars (same component, deeper call) keep the hue and step one tone darker. Folds keep the dashed neutral body but carry the stripe at the top, so a folded component is still recognisable. Ingress middleware hatch, 🔒 reference dashes, the New spark and Changed ring are unchanged and sit on top of the component colour.
  6. Never the only cue. The name is on every head and the step bar names both ends with their dots. The colour helps the eye; it carries no information the text doesn't.
  7. Arrows: option A (recommended). The bars already show who is at each end of an arrow, so tinting the line as well adds noise and costs the transport reading.
  8. Legend of a flow lists only the components in it, each with its dot.

Storage, safety and reach

  • Stored as a sanitized HTML subset: p br strong em s ul ol li a table thead tbody tr th td span, with only href (http, https, mailto) on links and a class from the palette on spans. The server strips everything else, so what is shown is what was stored, and the client trusts nothing.

Frame 18. Users and @mentions (new, bug #66)

Trigger: the Confluence importer brings review comments by “Einat Schanin” that mention “@Asaf Gilai”. Today an author is free text and a mention is plain text, so the import cannot say who wrote or who was addressed. Proposal (QA TL + lead): a small users registry, @mentions as chips, a Mentioned me view, and the same registry behind the action-item owner. No accounts or passwords: “who am I” is still a choice remembered on this browser.

  1. A user is a name, a colour and optional external ids. Avatar = initials on a tint of one of the eight component tones (--cmp-*, picked from a hash of the id, so it never changes with a rename). Users are created on first use: a comment author, an action-item owner, an import’s author or mention.
  2. A mention is a chip, stored as @[userId] in the body and drawn with the current display name, so a rename updates old comments. A chip for you is filled (accent); everyone else is a tinted pill. An id that no longer exists reads “@unknown user” in a dashed chip. Old plain “@Priya” text is left as text, never guessed.
  3. Imported authors keep their face and name and gain a small source mark (“C Confluence”). Hover or focus shows the external account id and where it came from.
  4. Where unread mentions live (my proposal): a global Mentions (3) entry in the left rail, next to Flows, opening a list across environments; plus a small “@ 1” badge on the flow’s row in the Flows list, and the Mentioned me filter in the Comments header. The rail entry is the one that answers “did anyone ask me something?” without knowing which flow. Unread = a mention newer than this viewer’s last visit to that comment thread (kept with the viewer, not in a shared table).
  5. The Users page sits under Settings in the rail. It lists, renames and merges. It never deletes: a user with history cannot vanish (that would orphan authors).

Rules and open questions (Frame 18)

  • Identity is not security. “Who am I” is a remembered choice. Anyone can pick anyone; “you” on a comment still means “the remembered user equals the author”. Real sign-in is out of scope.
  • Users are created on first use, by exact external id first (authorExternalId + source, so a rename in Confluence does not fork a user), else by name ignoring case and spacing, else new. The importer’s mentions work the same way, which is how “Asaf Gilai” appeared next to the old “Asaf G.”.
  • Storage. A users table (id, name, colour slot, created, last seen), user_external_ids (user, source, external id, unique together), user_aliases (user, alias). Comment author and action-item owner become a user id; the old free-text author is kept as author_name for display when the user is gone. The body keeps @[userId]; the server resolves nothing at write time except to check the id exists. MCP add_comment accepts author as a name or user id, and mentions as @[id] or @Name (matched, else left as text).
  • Merge rewrites the ids on comments, replies, action items, mentions in bodies and the unread bookkeeping, in one transaction, after a backup. It keeps the dropped name as an alias by default (proposal), so history reads naturally and imports that still send the old name resolve. Not undoable from the UI. Open: should merging be allowed to anyone, since there is no sign-in? Proposal: yes, with the backup as the safety net.
  • Rename changes only the display name; chips and authors follow because they store ids. The old name is not kept as an alias unless asked (a checkbox in the rename row, off).
  • Notifications beyond the in-app badge (email, Slack, push) are out of scope. The assistant reads mentions through MCP (list_mentions {user, unread}, proposal).
  • Unread bookkeeping is per user and per comment (a small table of “seen” marks), not a count on the user, so it survives a merge and an edit that adds a mention to an old comment (it becomes unread).
  • Editing a mention out of a comment drops the unread mark; a mention in a deleted comment disappears from the Mentions page.
  • Open for the maintainer: (1) Is the rail “Mentions” entry the right home, or should mentions only be a badge on Flows? (2) Should an avatar picture (URL) be settable now, or later as agreed? (3) Do harvested environments show mentions too (comments exist in every environment)? Drawn as yes. (4) Should the assistant (MCP) be a user with its own colour, drawn as “Assistant”? (5) May a mention be of a group (“@everyone”)? Proposal: no, not now.

Trigger: the importer doing HLD work wants to read, on the map and in a flow, what a component or a subsystem is: capabilities, why it exists, who owns it, what is still open. Aboutwritten in design, Oct 21

Capabilities
  • Compiles tenant policy documents into per-POP rule bundles.
  • Validates every rule against the schema and rejects conflicting ones with a reason.
  • Publishes a versioned bundle to policy-events.
Motivation

Policies used to be edited on each POP by hand, so two POPs could disagree for hours. One compiler gives one source of truth and a version number every POP can report back.

The old per-POP editor is kept for emergencies retired in Q3 (see the migration plan).

Roles and responsibilities
  • Owns rule semantics and the bundle format.
  • Does not distribute bundles: pop-policy-cache pulls them.
Owners

Platform Policy team. Ask Einat Schanin for format changes.

Rules and open questions (Frame 19)

  • Extend, not replace. One pane per selection; the old panel becomes “Connections” at the bottom. Edge, endpoint, step and queue panels are not changed. Component and subsystem are the only two kinds that get About.
  • Selection model. The pane follows the last click on a component, head, band or box. Clicking empty canvas closes it unless pinned. The URL carries sel=<component|subsystem> so a link opens the page with the pane (proposal; the flow view needs a way to say “head” vs “step”).
  • Keyboard. The pane is a labelled region. Opening it by keyboard moves focus to its title; Esc closes and returns focus to the node. In the editor, Esc asks before discarding.
  • Markdown-lite, sanitized at render, never at store. Headings (## and ###), lists, bold, italic, strike, inline code, links. Links must be http(s) or mailto and open in a new tab with noopener; anything else renders as text. Raw HTML and images are shown as text. The same renderer serves the pane, exports and MCP.
  • Section convention, not schema. The five names are matched ignoring case and a trailing colon. The pane only treats Open questions specially (tint and header count). Other headings render as plain blocks; order is the author’s.
  • Status comes from the design diff, not from the about. New, Changed (the about counts as a change of a prod component), Removed, Unchanged. In a harvested environment there is no status mark, only the “prod” label.
  • Edit scope. Only the about is edited in the pane. Docs links get a small inline form (main link, sub links) since #54 stores them as fields; team, kind and subsystem stay in Environment settings and the Inventory. Save to draft writes one design edit; two people editing the same about in one draft: last save wins with a “changed by someone else” prompt (proposal, same rule as comments).
  • Long abouts. The About block is never truncated in the pane. In row summaries (19b, Inventory) the first non-heading line is used, cut at about 90 characters.
  • Related flows are computed from calls, not stored: any flow whose steps touch the component; for a subsystem, any flow touching any of its components, with a count of components involved.
  • Open for the maintainer: (1) Is extending today’s panel right, or should About be its own pane and the old panel stay? (2) Pin off by default, or should the pane stay open after a click on empty canvas? (3) Should @mentions (frame 18) work inside the about, so Owners can be real users? I left them out of the markdown subset. (4) Should “Open questions” feed a global list (like comments on frame 6 / Inventory), so unanswered questions can be found across the design? (5) Can a harvested environment carry about through the manifest (shown in 19e as about: on the service)? (6) Per-subsystem owner is shown from free text in the about for now; do we want a structured field?
  • Not verified: real focus order, sheet drag behaviour, and rendering of very long unbroken words in the pane. The mockup is static.

Phases

  1. Environments and marks: envs table, copy from any source (Blank, Components, Full), subsystems, kind client, baseline and the computed New / Changed / Removed down to fields, frames 1, 2, 5, 7.
  2. Drafts and editing: drafts with apply and discard, panel forms, then direct editing on the diagram, flow names, import (frames 4, 6).

  4. Discussions: threads on any anchor, the assistant's replies, the all-discussions page, per-call AI descriptions (frames 4, 8).

Decided (rev 2)

  1. Copy from any environment, Full copy included in phase 1; the label is "From <source>".
  2. New is a small yellow spark, computed by the server by comparing with the baseline, on every level down to a field.
  3. Neighbours one hop.
  4. Discuss anything, plus an all-discussions view later; flows get names.
  5. Full editing through shared named drafts; sharing and invites stay naive (free-text author) with room for members later.
  6. Subsystem-level abstraction with refine and roll-up.
  7. Import on demand from any environment, by the assistant or by hand.
  8. References and import (rev 4, frame 10): a copied endpoint without its behaviour is a 🔒 reference; "Import into design" copies one level (its own flow and what it uses), never recursively; continuations stay locked and partial flows and edits across a reference say so.
  9. Every flow page (rev 3): a wide collapsible flow description under the title, numbered steps with a ‹ › walk and a wide step description above the diagram, Squeeze on by default.
